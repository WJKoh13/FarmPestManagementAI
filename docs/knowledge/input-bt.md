## Why it only affects caterpillars

Bt is a soil bacterium that produces crystal proteins. Those crystals only
become toxic when they are dissolved, and dissolving them requires the strongly
alkaline gut that caterpillars have and most other animals do not. Once
activated, the protein binds to specific receptors in the gut wall, the gut is
perforated, and the caterpillar stops feeding within hours and dies over a day
or two.

The requirement for both an alkaline gut and the matching receptor is why Bt is
so unusually selective. Beetles, aphids, bees, earthworms, birds and humans lack
one or both, which is why Bt is one of the few insecticides that can be used
without wrecking the beneficial insect population in the crop.

Different Bt subspecies target different orders. The one sold for caterpillars
does not work on beetle larvae, and vice versa — buying the wrong subspecies is
a common and completely invisible reason for failure.

## It must be eaten

Bt is a stomach poison with no contact activity at all. A caterpillar that walks
through a treated patch is unharmed; it has to eat treated leaf. Three things
follow from that.

Spray where the caterpillars are feeding, which is often the underside of leaves
or deep in the whorl of a cereal. Spray while they are small, because young
larvae eat proportionally more surface leaf and are far more susceptible. And
accept that any caterpillar already tunnelled inside a stem, a fruit or a rolled
leaf will never encounter the spray — for borers, the window is the short period
after hatching and before entry.

## It does not last

Sunlight breaks Bt down quickly, typically within a day or two of application.
This is good for residues and bad for planning: it means Bt has essentially no
persistence, so it only controls the larvae present when you spray. Eggs that
hatch afterwards are unaffected.

That short life is also why Bt is best matched to a scouting programme. Applied
on a calendar it mostly misses; applied when monitoring shows a hatch, it works
well.

## Stopping feeding comes before dying

Treated caterpillars stop eating almost immediately but can remain visibly alive
for a day or more. Crop damage stops long before the caterpillars disappear, so
judging the result by how many are still moving will consistently understate it.
Always follow the product label and the rules of your certification body.
